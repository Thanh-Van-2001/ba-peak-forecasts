Do Grid Operators Under-Forecast Peak Load? Selection, Heat and a Decade of Day-Ahead Forecasts in U.S. Balancing Authorities

Public scorecards report that the day-ahead load forecasts of U.S. balancing authorities (BAs) are below demand at the hour of the daily peak on about 60% of days. Evaluating forecasts at times chosen by the outcome favours forecasts biased toward extremes (the forecaster's dilemma). With a pre-specified plan and EIA-930 data for 43 BAs and 164 thousand BA-days, 2016 to 2026, we compare the median error of the daily peak on the 5% of days with the highest actual peak with that on the 5% of days with the highest forecast peak. On actual-peak days the forecast was low in 32 of 43 BAs (Holm p = 0.004); on forecast-peak days in only 18 (p = 0.89). Measured by the daily maximum rather than at the actual peak hour, the forecast was low on 54% of BA-days. In exploratory analyses, forecasts on forecast-peak days exceeded each BA's usual bias in 35 of 43 BAs. We find no evidence of a systematic under-forecast of peak days; the scorecard figure reflects selection. On days above the 95th percentile of summer temperature, the forecast was lower than on other summer days in 30 of 43 BAs (Holm p = 0.02; pooled −0.56 percentage points). Exploratory analyses place this shortfall on the first day of a hot spell; on the following day the forecast was not low. Summer-day accuracy did not change between 2016–2019 and 2023–2026. Scorecards should condition on the forecast, and operators should watch the onset of heat.

Keywords: Load forecasting, peak demand, forecast evaluation, heat waves, EIA-930.
